## Abstract

T-cell receptor (TCR) repertoire sequencing allows researchers to analyze millions of TCRs, providing unparalleled precision in understanding immune responses and enabling broad applications. However, existing TCR-related databases are based on a limited number of samples. Here, we present TCRdb2.0 (https://guolab.wchscu.cn/TCRdb2/#/), an updated and significantly expanded resource with enriched data and enhanced functionalities. TCRdb2.0 incorporates ∼700 million TCR sequences derived from 19 701 TCR-Seq samples across 46 tissues and 147 clinical conditions, making it the most comprehensive TCR sequence database to date. The homepage of TCRdb2.0 has powerful browsing, searching and downloading functions. It displays multiple features of TCR in sample and project levels. Compared to the previous release, TCRdb2.0 has the following major improvements: (i) a substantial increase of TCR sequences, from 277 million to ∼700 million; (ii) inclusion of TCR sequences from Gamma delta (γδ) T cells; (iii) integration of therapy-related TCR-Seq datasets, such as programmed cell death 1 (PD-1) blockade immunotherapy; (iv) construction of the largest TCR sequence reference from healthy samples; and (v) redesign of a new search and download function, enabling flexible queries and downloads. With its extensive data and user-friendly web interface, TCRdb2.0 will serve as an invaluable resource for functional studies of TCRs in both health and disease.

## Introduction

The T-cell receptor (TCR) is a protein complex on T-cell surfaces that recognizes and binds to specific antigens, triggering a series of events that activate and proliferate T cells [1]. The TCR repertoire represents the entire set of TCRs generated by an individual, and it constitutes the essential component in human adaptive immune system. The diversity of TCRs primarily arises from V(D)J recombination, a somatic reorganization process that leads to significant variation in the complementarity-determining region 3 (CDR3) of the receptor [2]. It is postulated that an individual may possess up to 1016 unique TCR specificities due to this mechanism [3].

The considerable heterogeneity within TCR repertoires holds significant potential for disease diagnostics and therapeutic interventions. Growing interest in this field has produced extensive datasets, which have driven the development of advanced analytical techniques [4–6]. Despite the abundance of publicly available TCR-Seq datasets, only a fraction of these has been fully utilized and collated by existing databases. Most current TCR databases are specialized and primarily focus on data from low-throughput, experimentally validated assays. For example, VDJdb [7], IEDB [8], and McPAS-TCR [9], curated TCR sequences linked to pathogens or antigens, comprising ∼100 000 TCR sequences. NeoTCR [10] collected experimentally supported functional neoantigen-specific TCR sequences. iRecetpor [11] and immuneACCESS (https://clients.adaptivebiotech.com/immuneaccess) required registration. These databases lack detailed information about TCR repertoires and features with user-unfriendly web interfaces.

In 2021, we developed TCRdb (TCRdb1.0), a comprehensive resource that contains ∼277 million TCR sequences from over 8000 samples [12]. Since then, the continuous generation of TCR-Seq datasets has provided valuable insights into the functional potential of TCRs [13]. This rapid data accumulation has highlighted the urgent need for unified aggregation and analysis pipelines to better characterize the TCR repertoire. To address this need, we updated our database to TCRdb2.0, which now contains 269 projects, 19 701 samples, and nearly 700 million reliable sequences spanning 46 tissues and 147 clinical conditions. TCRdb2.0 is designed to provide human TCR sequences associated with specific tissues, clinical conditions, and cell types through a robust and versatile search system, including a newly added bulk TCR search function. We believe that TCRdb2.0 will serve as a valuable resource for the immunology community and facilitate a wide range of studies on TCR function.

## Materials and methods

### Data collection and processing

In the study, we focused on the TCR beta, gamma, and delta chains due to their relative significance [14–16], while excluding the alpha chain. We downloaded TCR-Seq data or TCR sequences of 19 701 samples from (i) the NCBI Sequence Read Archive (SRA) through the keywords “TCR-Seq,” “TCR,” “Homo sapiens,” “T-cell receptor” and (ii) other TCR sequence databases such as immuneACCESS (https://clients.adaptivebiotech.com/immuneaccess). The metadata of TCR-Seq datasets (including but not limited to tissue, cell type, and clinical conditions) was manually curated based on the descriptions provided by their source websites or publications. In cases where conflicts arose between the metadata from publications and database descriptions, precedence was given to the information from the literature. Furthermore, to address potential errors in manual annotations, we provide links to the original data for each entry and encourage the community users to report any discrepancies in the database. We will correct problematic data accordingly.

TCR-Seq data from SRA were downloaded through SRA-toolkit (version 3.1.0). Fastp (version 0.23.4) [17] was used to perform quality control, excluding some low-quality reads for reducing potential singleton and error TCRs. A Q25 standard was applied, with the first and last 5 bp of each read being discarded if their quality fell below 25. For data labeled with Unique Molecular Identifiers (UMIs), UMI-tools [18] were employed to remove barcodes and subsequently correct reads with identical UMIs. In cases where UMI information could not be determined from the study description, we inferred the potential UMI region based on read conservation and directly removed it from each read, then treated the sample as a non-UMI labeled sample. Finally, we employed MiXCR (version 4.1.0) [19] to extract TCR sequences. For TCR sequences from other TCR databases, we manually downloaded them and re-organized the data to fit the structure of TCRdb2.0.

### TCR sequence quality control

All TCR sequences obtained from raw TCR-Seq data and other TCR sequence databases were performed additional refinement to ensure their authenticity and high quality. Only intact TCR sequences were preserved, with consistent V gene, J gene, and in-frame CDR3 sequences. The CDR3 amino acid sequence was required to start with cysteine (C) and terminate with phenylalanine (F) or tryptophan (W), aligning with the criteria established by the international ImMunoGeneTics information system (IMGT) [20], while being devoid of any termination codons. Different alleles of the same V/J gene will be merged, and only the gene information will be retained. Following this consolidation, we calculated the proportion of each TCR sequence within the repertoire.

### Database construction

The TCRdb2.0 was developed using Vue (version 2.6.1), a JavaScript-based web interface framework and Flask (version 1.0.3), a python-based web application framework. The current version of TCRdb2 runs on an Apache web server deployed on an Ubuntu Linux Server. All datasets were documented and managed in MongoDB (version 4.2.1), stored across three levels: “Project,” “Sample,” and “Sequence.” We utilized G2 (version 4.0.0) and Echarts (version 4.6.0) for creating interactive visualizations.

### Data summary and overall functions

TCRdb2.0 is a comprehensive TCR sequence database, containing 269 projects, 19 701 samples, and nearly 700 million reliable sequences across 46 tissues, and 147 clinical conditions. The data volume is double than the TCRdb1.0. The detailed comparison between TCRdb1.0 and TCRdb2.0 can be found in Table 1, while Table 2 lists the comparison between TCRdb2.0 and other databases. All sequences were organized into a three-layer structure: project, sample, and sequence levels. The main modules in TCRdb2.0 are “Search,” “Browse,” “Health,” “Dataset,” and “Download” modules. In the “Search” module, users can flexibly query the database through three search functions, which are “Batch search,” “Advanced search,” and “Healthy search.” These functions propose new approaches for assessing the specificity of TCR sequences. The “Browse” module allows users to explore the data across multiple dimensions (disease, tissue, cell type, and treatment), facilitating cross-dataset comparisons. The “Health” model contains 1888 samples whose clinical condition was labeled as healthy controls, providing a valuable reference for baseline immune repertoire analysis. The “Dataset” module includes all projects in the database. Most importantly, the “Download” module provides a convenient way for users to access and download data of interest for downstream analyses (Fig. 1).

## Improved content and new features

### New search function

TCRdb2.0 offers three powerful search functions in the “Search” module that enable users to query TCR sequences of interest in the database (Supplementary Fig. S1). Given the high diversity and disease specificity of the beta chain, TCRdb2.0 currently supports searches for the beta chain only. The first is “Healthy search” function, which allows users to retrieve or filter TCRs in healthy samples. The second is the “Batch search,” a highly valuable tool that enables users to query up to 2000 CDR3 sequences simultaneously and retrieve records with matching CDR3 sequences. The third is the “Advanced search” which offers two distinct modes, “Fuzzy search” and “Regex search,” to meet diverse querying needs. The “Fuzzy search” can identify CDR3 sequences in the database that differ from the query sequence by fewer than two amino acids, including amino acid insertions/deletions. This feature allows browsing of CDR3 sequences of varying lengths and facilitates the discovery of similar sequences, providing additional insights into related sequences even when the original query is absent in the database. The “Regex search” allows users to explore sequences with specific characteristics by inputting a regular expression, substring, or sequence pattern. This approach is particularly useful for identifying sequence groups that may exhibit sample-specific features either within or across datasets. Additionally, TCRdb2.0 provides several visualizations to illustrate the outcomes of the search function, allowing users to explore V or/and J gene usage, as well as the distribution of resulting sequences across various clinical conditions, tissues, and cell types. The “Batch search” and “Fuzzy search” are powered by Elasticsearch (https://www.elastic.co/elasticsearch), an open-source distributed search and analytics engine, to enable fast sequence search.

### Expansion of TCR-Seq data from γδ T cells

Gamma delta (γδ) T cells are an unconventional T-cell subset that play critical roles in various immune responses and immunopathology [21]. The γδ TCR is the unique receptor expressed on γδ T cells, capable of recognizing a wide range of antigens in a Major Histocompatibility Complex (MHC) independent manner [22]. In TCRdb2.0, we have included a total of 1 235 samples of TCRδ and TCRγ chains from 17 projects for users to browse and utilize. These samples span various clinical conditions, including autoimmune disease, virus, and healthy control, and can be accessed through both the “Browse” and “Dataset” modules.

### Construction of the largest TCR reference in healthy samples

Numerous studies focus on identifying tumor-antigen-associated TCRs by removing sequences in non-cancer control samples [23, 24]. Currently, the most frequently used healthy TCR dataset is from the study by Emerson et al. (2017) [25]. However, due to the diversity of TCRs, these datasets are far from sufficient to cover the necessary variability. In TCRdb2.0, we curated a comprehensive healthy TCR sequence reference by collecting 1888 samples labeled as healthy controls from 40 published studies. This resulted in a dataset comprising 258 620 356 sequences, making it the largest collection of healthy TCR sequences to date. Users can download the entire dataset directly via the “Download” button in the “Health” page. To further enhance usability, we further developed a built-in function that allows retrieval or filtering of healthy antigen-associated sequences within a sample. The “Health” page provides more detailed information about data sources, age distribution, and gender composition.

### Integration of therapy-related TCR-Seq datasets

Tumor immunotherapy directly influences the number, type, and functional state of T cells in the immune system through multiple mechanisms [26]. Tracking changes in TCR during treatment can detect tumor-specific T cells and discover potential biomarkers that can predict treatment response [27]. In TCRdb2.0, we manually curated and annotated detailed clinical treatment information for all projects, and categorized them according to different therapeutic approaches. In total of 16 projects containing treatment-related information were identified, comprising 1413 samples. The treatment modalities include PD-1/cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated antigen-4 (CTLA-4) blockade immunotherapy, radiotherapy, adoptive T-cell therapy and others. These results are accessible through the “Browse” and “Dataset” modules.

### Web interface and modules of TCRdb2.0

TCRdb2.0 offers users versatile navigation capabilities, enabling efficient exploration of the database. It includes five main functional modules: “Browse,” “Dataset,” “Health,” “Download,” and “Search” modules. The “Browse” module allows users to browse the database through various categories including tissues, diseases, treatment, and cell types (Fig. 2A). The “Dataset” module allows users to browse all datasets and filter them by project ID, cell type, tissue type, and clinical condition, enhancing the ease of finding relevant data (Fig. 2B). In the “Health” module, users can browse collected healthy projects and directly download the healthy reference, which can serve as a negative dataset for disease-related TCR studies (Fig. 2C). To facilitate personalized and efficient use of data, the “Download” module allows users to download projects by filtering specific categories or diseases (Fig. 2D). The “Search” module integrates three search functions: “Batch search,” “Healthy search,” and “Advanced search,” allowing users to search TCR sequence through multiple approaches (Fig. 2E). In addition to these five modules, TCRdb2.0 also provides dynamics figures to visualize the TCR repertoire characteristics on the “Project,” “Sample,” and “Sequence” pages for user convenience (Supplementary Fig. S2).

### Summary and future perspectives

In this work, we present TCRdb2.0, an updated version of TCRdb. This database collected TCR sequences with annotated metadata by manually curated from public TCR-Seq data and databases. TCRdb2.0 contains 269 TCR-Seq studies, comprising 19 701 samples and 691 744 135 sequences associated with diverse diseases, cell types, and tissues. All datasets in TCRdb2.0 are processed by a uniform pipeline to ensure the sequences reliability. As of now, TCRdb2.0 is the most comprehensive TCR sequence database available. TCRdb2.0 supports a variety of applications. For example, identifying tumor-specific T-cell clones is critical for TCR-T therapy [28]. Users can query candidate TCR sequences using “Search” module and filter out public sequences. Additionally, the function in “Health” module allows users to filter out healthy antigen-associated sequences from TCR repertoires. Moreover, the healthy sequences dataset can serves as a valuable negative dataset for training machine learning models in disease diagnosis [29].

While TCRdb2.0 has collected nearly all publicly available TCR-Seq data, it is still far from sufficient. Going forward, we aim to not only build upon the functionality of TCRdb2.0 but also expand the number of collected datasets. We are committed to ongoing maintenance and enhancement, with plans to expand by including additional TCR data, such as (i) TCR sequences from paired chain; (ii) TCR sequences from other kinds of sequencing data, including single-cell sequencing data; (iii) TCR-Seq data from various treatment methods; (iv) TCR sequences from other species, such as Mus musculus and Sus scrofa; (v) Antigen-specific TCR sequences validated by experiments. TCRdb2.0 will be a comprehensive resource for cancer immune-related researchers and will provide new insights into immunotherapy.
